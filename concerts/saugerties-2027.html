Masked Sounds

April 18, 2027 · 3:00 pm · Saugerties, New York

Two of these five pieces were written for a flute and three were not. Haydn's is one of the two: his London publisher John Bland called on him at Eszterháza in November 1789, and by the summer of 1790 Bland had three trios for flute, cello and piano in print - the flute because it was the instrument English amateurs actually owned. Haydn took them with him on his first journey to London. At a little over twenty minutes this one is also the longest work on the programme.

In the Mendelssohn the flute takes the violin's line. Mendelssohn finished the D minor Trio in September 1839 and then rewrote the entire piano part, having been told by Ferdinand Hiller that it was too plain; what came back was far more demanding, and it is the version everyone plays. Schumann reviewed it as "the master-trio of our time," ranking it with Beethoven and Schubert, and called Mendelssohn "the Mozart of the nineteenth century." The first two movements are heard here: the Molto allegro agitato and the Andante that follows it.

Copland is the other composer here who wrote for the flute, and he wrote it as a memorial. After the death of the Philadelphia flutist William Kincaid in 1967, seventy of his pupils and friends commissioned a piece in his memory; Copland finished the Duo in 1971, and Elaine Shaffer and Hephzibah Menuhin gave the premiere in Philadelphia on 3 October that year. Its three movements are marked Flowing, Poetic somewhat mournful, and Lively with bounce. The elegy for Kincaid is the middle one.

Dumky is the plural of dumka, a Ukrainian word for a lament of a captive people, and Dvořák wrote six of them, each turning between mourning and dancing. The first three run without a break, in effect one long opening movement; this programme takes the first two. Dvořák gave the premiere in Prague on 11 April 1891, then played the piece on a forty-concert farewell tour of Bohemia and Moravia before sailing for New York to direct the National Conservatory.

Ravel's Kaddisch was written for a voice, not an instrument at all. It opens the Deux mélodies hébraïques of 1914, both built on traditional Hebrew melodies, and the Kaddisch is the prayer for the dead: a long unaccompanied-sounding cantilena over a held pedal note. Alvina Alvi sang the first performance on 3 June 1914 with Ravel at the piano. He orchestrated the pair five years later. On a flute the line loses its words and keeps its shape.

About CreArtBox

CreArtBox is a New York–based chamber music and multimedia ensemble founded in 2013, performing at The DiMenna Center for Classical Music and on tour internationally. The ensemble's New York Series presents approximately six productions a year - combining commissioned premieres, reimagined classics, and collaborations with visual artists, filmmakers, and writers.

In parallel, CreArtBox runs Festival ADAR each August in rural Asturias, Spain - an international arts festival pairing chamber music with site-specific performance in churches, hórreos, and mountain villages. Both the New York Series and Festival ADAR are produced by CreArtBox, Inc., a 501(c)(3) non-profit.
